You found the paradox

The shortcut is each driver’s best option—and everyone’s worse outcome.

The whole town Before road: 65 min · After choices: 80 min

Everyone is 15 minutes slower than before the shortcut existed.

The shortcut is not magically slow. It changes everyone’s reason for choosing a route. Each person follows the quickest option available to them, which sends all 4,000 drivers through both narrow roads. Removing the shortcut forces traffic to split again, clearing those roads and returning every trip to 65 minutes. This is Braess’s paradox: individually sensible choices can create a worse result for the whole group.
